*Cleaning, linen and turnover services for Airbnb and short-term rental hosts*

## 1. Executive Summary

**The business.** FreshTurn is a property care company for short-term rental (STR) hosts. We do **not** manage listings, set prices or talk to guests. We handle the physical work between bookings:

- Turnover cleaning after every checkout
- Linen and towel supply and laundry
- Restocking consumables (toiletries, coffee, paper goods)
- Photo-verified inspections and damage reports
- Light maintenance and coordination of trades
- Deep cleans and new-listing setup

**The problem.** Hosts who self-manage to avoid the 20–30% fees of full-service property managers still have to solve checkout day. Ordinary residential cleaners don't fit it: they don't work weekends or same-day windows, they don't restock or report damage, and they don't sync to booking calendars. A single missed or poor clean can cost a host a 5-star rating, and with it their search ranking.

**The solution.** A reliable, calendar-synced turnover service with a hotel-style checklist and a photo report after every clean. It's priced per turnover so hosts can pass the cost straight through in their Airbnb cleaning fee.

**The opportunity.** Every booked night at an STR eventually needs a turnover. A typical active listing generates 6–10 turnovers a month, which makes this recurring, predictable revenue with high customer stickiness: once a host trusts their cleaner, they rarely switch.

**Financial snapshot (base case):**

**Startup capital required:** ~$17,000 (lean version ~$13,000, see section 9).
**Break-even:** ~7 active properties (about 48 turnovers a month).

## 3. Services & Pricing

### 3.1 Core services

| Service | Description |
|---|---|
| **Turnover clean** | Full clean between guests, following a room-by-room checklist. Beds made, kitchen reset, bathrooms sanitized, trash out, staging done to the host's photos. |
| **Linens & laundry** | We own and rotate commercial-grade linen sets (3 sets per bed: one on the bed, one in the wash, one spare). This removes the host's biggest headache. |
| **Restocking** | Consumables topped up every visit, billed at cost + 10%. |
| **Inspection & photo report** | 15–30 timestamped photos per clean, plus a checklist and notes. Damage and missing-item reports in the format AirCover claims need. |
| **Maintenance** | Small fixes on the spot (bulbs, batteries, loose fittings). Bigger issues flagged and booked with vetted trades for a 10–15% coordination fee. |
| **Deep clean / reset** | Quarterly or seasonal: ovens, fridges, baseboards, windows, upholstery. Also offered as setup for new listings. |

### 3.2 Pricing (per turnover)

| Size | Turnover | + Linens | Deep clean |
|---|---|---|---|
| Studio | $85 | +$20 | $220 |
| 1 bed | $105 | +$25 | $260 |
| 2 bed | $135 | +$40 | $320 |
| 3 bed | $175 | +$55 | $400 |
| 4+ bed | from $220 | +$70 | from $500 |

**Add-ons:** extra bathroom +$20 · hot tub / pool check +$25 · same-day rush (<4 hrs notice) +25% · restocking at cost + 10% · laundry-only service $2.50/lb.

**Plans:**
- **Pay as you go:** standard rates, no commitment.
- **Full Care (recommended):** turnovers + linens + restocking + monthly maintenance check. 5% off every clean, 20% off quarterly deep cleans, priority scheduling.
- **Portfolio (5+ properties / co-hosts):** custom rates (typically 8–12% off), dedicated account manager, consolidated invoicing.

**Pricing logic:** a 2-bed turnover takes about 2.5 labor hours. At $135 + $40 linens = $175 per turnover, a host charging a $150–$185 Airbnb cleaning fee covers our cost almost fully. That's the key sales message: *"This costs you close to nothing. Your guests pay for it."*

## 4. Market Analysis

### 4.1 Target customers

| Segment | Description | Why they buy | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Self-managing hosts (1–3 listings)** | Own their listing, often have a day job or live elsewhere | Time, reliability, distance from the property | ★★★ Primary |
| **Small co-hosts / boutique managers (4–30 listings)** | Run listings for owners but don't want to employ cleaners | Need scale, consistency and reporting | ★★★ Primary (biggest accounts) |
| **Remote / out-of-town investors** | Bought in a tourist area, live hours away | Need eyes on the property | ★★ |
| **Serviced apartment & boutique hotel operators** | Small operators without in-house housekeeping | Overflow and weekend coverage | ★ Later |

### 4.2 Market sizing (method to apply locally)

1. Pull your metro's active STR listing count from AirDNA, Inside Airbnb or AllTheRooms.
2. **Serviceable market** = listings within your drive radius × % professionally cleaned (assume 60–70%).
3. Revenue pool = serviceable listings × avg. turnovers/month (≈ 7) × avg. ticket (≈ $155) × 12.

*Example:* a city with 3,000 active listings → ~2,000 professionally cleaned → 2,000 × 7 × $155 × 12 ≈ **$26M/year** in turnover spend. The Year 3 target of 80 properties is **4%** of that serviceable market.

### 4.3 Industry trends

- STR supply keeps growing in most leisure and urban markets, and more hosts self-manage using tools like Hospitable, Hostaway and Guesty.
- Guest reviews weight cleanliness heavily, and Airbnb ranks listings partly on reviews, so hosts are willing to pay for consistency.
- Tighter local STR regulation in some cities reduces listing counts but pushes the remaining hosts to professionalize, which is good for us.
- Turnover-scheduling marketplaces (e.g. Turno) prove demand but supply individual cleaners of varying quality. A branded, accountable company is the premium alternative.

### 4.4 Competition

| Competitor type | Strengths | Weaknesses | Our edge |
|---|---|---|---|
| Independent cleaners (Turno, Facebook groups) | Cheap, flexible | No-shows, no backup, inconsistent quality, no insurance | Team backup, insurance, checklists, photo proof |
| Residential cleaning companies | Established, insured | Weekday-only, no linens/restocking, no calendar sync | Built only for STR turnovers |
| Full-service property managers | One-stop shop | Take 20–30% of revenue; host loses control | Host keeps control and pays per turnover |
| Host does it themselves | Free | Time, burnout, can't scale | Our cost is passed through to guests |

## 6. Operations Plan

### 6.1 How a turnover works

1. Booking lands on the host's calendar and syncs via iCal to our scheduling software.
2. The job is auto-assigned to a cleaner based on zone and availability; the cleaner confirms in the app.
3. Checkout (usually 10–11 AM): cleaner arrives with a linen kit and supply caddy.
4. Clean is done against a digital checklist with required photos per room.
5. Restock, then a final staging check against the host's reference photos.
6. Photo report is sent automatically; any issues are escalated to the ops lead.
7. Dirty linens go to the laundry (in-house machines or commercial laundry partner) and are back in rotation within 24–48 hours.

### 6.2 Standards & quality control

- Standard checklist per property, plus host-specific notes (codes, quirks, staging photos).
- Required photos: every room, inside the fridge, under the beds, the bathrooms, and the outdoor areas.
- **Spot inspections:** ops lead re-inspects 10% of cleans in Year 1, 5% after that.
- **Service guarantee:** if the host or guest reports an issue within 24 hours, we return and fix it free.
- **KPI targets:** 99%+ on-time completion · <2% re-clean rate · average host rating 4.8+.

### 6.3 Technology stack

| Need | Tool options | Est. cost |
|---|---|---|
| Scheduling & calendar sync | Turno, Breezeway, Properly, or Jobber + iCal | $50–$300/mo |
| Checklists & photo reports | Built into Breezeway/Properly/Turno | Included |
| Invoicing & payments | QuickBooks Online + Stripe | $35/mo + fees |
| Payroll | Gusto | $50–$150/mo |
| Website & forms | This site + Netlify/Vercel hosting + Formspree | $0–$20/mo |
| Comms | Google Workspace, business phone (OpenPhone) | $25/mo |

### 6.4 Linen operation

- Commercial white linens (easy to bleach and standardize), 3 sets per bed.
- Year 1: in-house washing (home or a small rented space with 2 commercial washers and dryers) or a commercial laundry partner at ~$1.25–$1.75/lb.
- Year 2+: dedicated laundry space once linen volume justifies it (~40+ properties).
- Track sets per property; replace about 30% of inventory a year from wear and stains.

### 6.5 Supplies (per cleaning kit)

Vacuum (cordless + full size), mop system, microfiber cloth system (color-coded by room), steamer, caddy, commercial-grade multi-surface, bathroom, glass and disinfectant products, gloves, trash bags, lint roller, inventory checklist. Supply cost averages **~$6 per turnover**.

## 7. Team & Staffing

### 7.1 Growth path

| Stage | Properties | Team |
|---|---|---|
| **Launch (months 0–3)** | 0–8 | Founder cleans + 1–2 part-time cleaners |
| **Growth (months 4–12)** | 8–30 | Founder moves to ops/sales; 4–6 cleaners |

| **Year 3** | 50–100 | Ops manager + field supervisor/inspector; 15–25 cleaners; dedicated laundry |

### 7.2 Hiring & pay

- **Pay:** $22–$26/hour (above local residential cleaning rates) plus a $5 bonus per clean rated 5 stars. Above-market pay is our main tool against cleaner turnover, which is the #1 operational risk in this industry.
- **Employees vs. contractors:** start with W-2 employees (or follow your local equivalent) where you control schedules, uniforms and checklists, since misclassifying contractors is a real legal risk. Budget ~15% on top of wages for payroll taxes and workers' comp.
- **Screening:** background check, paid trial clean, reference check.
- **Training:** 2 shadow shifts + checklist certification before working solo.
- **Retention:** consistent zones and properties, guaranteed weekly hours for core staff, mileage reimbursement.

## 11. Legal, Insurance & Compliance

- **Business registration:** LLC, local business license, EIN / tax registration.
- **Insurance:** general liability ($1–2M), janitorial bond (covers theft claims), workers' compensation once you have employees, commercial auto if staff drive for work, and inland marine / equipment coverage for linens and kits.
- **Client service agreement:** scope, pricing, cancellation (14 days' notice), late-cancellation fee (50% if cancelled within 24 hours), access/key responsibility, damage liability limits, payment terms (net 7–15, card on file).
- **Key & access policy:** prefer smart locks with unique cleaner codes. Physical keys go in a lockbox log and are never labeled with addresses.
- **Data & privacy:** don't photograph guests' personal belongings beyond what damage reporting needs; store access codes securely.
- **Employment law:** proper worker classification, minimum wage and overtime compliance, and paid travel time between jobs where local law requires it.
- **Local STR rules:** stay aware of your city's STR regulations. Our business is cleaning, but client churn follows regulation changes.

## 12. Risks & Mitigation

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cleaner no-show / staff turnover | High | High | Above-market pay, bench of on-call cleaners, founder as backup, cross-train on each property |
| Seasonality (slow off-season) | High | Medium | Target year-round urban/business listings, sell deep cleans in the off-season, flex hours |
| Same-day crunch (many checkouts on Sat/Sun) | High | Medium | Weekend-heavy scheduling, peak-day staffing, rush surcharge |
| Damage or theft accusations | Medium | High | Photo reports, janitorial bond, background checks, key logs |
| Client concentration (one big co-host leaves) | Medium | High | Cap any client at 25% of revenue, written agreements |
| Local STR regulation tightens | Medium | High | Diversify into mid-term rentals, serviced apartments and move-out cleans |
| Price competition from marketplace cleaners | Medium | Medium | Compete on reliability, proof and linens, not price |
| Late payment | Low | Medium | Card on file, auto-charge on invoice date |
