## 1. Executive Summary

**The business.** FreshTurn cleans Airbnb and holiday let properties in Bath between guests. We do **not** manage listings, set prices or talk to guests. We handle the physical work: cleaning, beds and towels, restocking, and a photo report after every clean.

**The model.** The founder runs the business: winning clients, scheduling, quality control and billing. The cleaning is done by **self-employed cleaners the founder already knows and trusts**, paid a flat fee per job. That means:

- almost no startup cost (cleaners bring their own equipment)
- no wages to pay, so costs only exist when there's paid work
- the founder's time goes into growing the business, not cleaning bathrooms

**Why Bath.** Bath is one of the UK's busiest tourist cities, with a large number of Airbnbs and holiday lets close together. That means lots of potential clients and short drives between jobs.

**The problem we solve.** Hosts who manage their own listings still have to deal with changeover day. Regular cleaners often don't work weekends or same-day windows, don't restock or report damage, and nobody covers for them when they're ill. One bad clean can cost a host a 5-star review.

**The solution.** A reliable, calendar-synced changeover service with a checklist, a photo report after every clean, and a backup cleaner always available. It's priced per clean so hosts can cover it with the cleaning fee their guests already pay.

**Status:** first client agreed (Bath).

**Financial snapshot (base case):**

UK cleaning margins are thinner than in some countries. In year 1 this is a solid side income. It becomes a real business once you add linen service and pass about 15–20 properties.

## 2. How the Business Works

```
 Host books a guest on Airbnb / Booking.com
          │
          ▼
 Calendar syncs to Turno ──► Clean job created automatically for changeover day
          │
          ▼
 Cleaner gets the job on their phone and accepts it
          │
          ▼
 Cleaner cleans between checkout (≈11am) and check-in (≈4pm)
 following the checklist, then uploads photos
          │
          ▼
 Host receives the photo report automatically
          │
          ▼
 End of month: FreshTurn invoices host ──► FreshTurn pays cleaners weekly
```

## 3. Services & Pricing

### 3.1 What hosts get

| Service | Description |
|---|---|
| **Changeover clean** | Full clean after every checkout: kitchen, bathrooms, floors, beds made hotel-style, bins emptied, everything put back like the host's listing photos. |
| **Laundry** | *Phase 1:* cleaners wash the host's own linen and towels on site (most lets have a washing machine). *Phase 2:* FreshTurn supplies its own linen sets for an extra fee (see 3.2). |
| **Restocking** | Toilet roll, soap, coffee and similar items topped up, billed at cost + 10%. |
| **Photo report** | 15–30 photos after every clean, plus any damage or missing items. That's the evidence hosts need for Airbnb damage claims. |
| **Deep clean** | Ovens, fridges, skirting boards, windows. Offered seasonally or before a new listing goes live. |

### 3.2 Prices (what the host pays)

| Size | Changeover clean | + FreshTurn linen (Phase 2) | Deep clean |
|---|---|---|---|
| Studio | £45 | +£12 | £140 |
| 1 bed | £50 | +£14 | £170 |
| 2 bed | £60 | +£26 | £210 |
| 3 bed | £80 | +£38 | £260 |
| 4+ bed | from £100 | +£50 | from £320 |

**Extras:** extra bathroom +£10 · hot tub check +£15 · same-day rush (less than 4 hours' notice) +25% · restocking at cost + 10%.

**How these compare:** across the UK, hosts typically pay about £40–£70 for a 2-bed changeover and Airbnb cleans average around £55. Bath also has cheap student cleaners at about £21 per changeover, so don't try to be the cheapest. Sell reliability, backup cover and photo reports.

**Pricing a new property:** estimate the hours, then charge about **£24 an hour** (rounded to a clean number). Time the first 4 cleans and adjust.

### 3.3 What cleaners are paid (flat per job)

| Size | Typical time | Cleaner pay | FreshTurn keeps (before supplies and fees) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Studio | 1.5–2 hrs | £30 | £15 |
| 1 bed | 2 hrs | £33 | £17 |
| 2 bed | 2.5 hrs | £40 | £20 |
| 3 bed | 3–3.5 hrs | £52 | £28 |
| 4+ bed | 4 hrs+ | £65+ | £35+ |
| Deep clean | — | 60% of price | 40% |
| Extra bathroom / rush | — | 50% of the extra | 50% |

**Why these rates work:** £40 for a 2-bed that takes 2.5 hours is £16 an hour. That's in line with what self-employed cleaners in the UK charge, and the jobs come to them without any effort. **Ask your cleaners what they charge now** and set pay so they feel it's a good deal. Their rate sets your minimum price.

**Selling point to hosts:** *"Our clean costs about the same as the cleaning fee your guests already pay, so it costs you almost nothing."*

## 5. Getting Clients

### 5.1 Channels, in order of how well they'll work at the start

1. **Your first client.** Do an excellent job, then ask for a Google review and introductions to other hosts they know.
2. **People you already know.** Friends, family and neighbours who rent out places, or who know someone who does.
3. **Local host Facebook groups.** Search "Bath Airbnb hosts" and "Bath holiday let owners". Be helpful and post your offer occasionally (see `templates/outreach-scripts.md`).
4. **Your cleaners' existing clients.** Some of the people they already clean for may own lets.
5. **Google Business Profile.** Free. It makes you show up for "Airbnb cleaning Bath". Ask every happy client for a review.
6. **The website.** Gives you credibility when hosts look you up, and lets them get a price and contact you.
7. **Partners (later).** Estate agents, letting agents, mortgage brokers who work with holiday let buyers, and local handymen. Offer £25–£50 for every client they send.

### 5.2 Sales process

1. Reply to every enquiry the same day.
2. Visit the property for 20–30 minutes (free) and take reference photos of how it should look.
3. Send a quote within 24 hours.
4. **First clean at 50% off** so trying you is risk-free.
5. Connect their calendar, then send a photo report after every clean.
6. After 30 days, ask for a Google review and a referral.

## 6. Operations

### 6.1 Tools

| Need | Tool | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Scheduling, calendar sync, checklists, photos | **Turno** (lets you add your own cleaners) | about £10–£40/month |
| Invoicing and payments | Zoho Invoice or Wave (free), or Xero / QuickBooks | Free – £20/month + card fees |
| Paying cleaners | Bank transfer from a business account (e.g. Starling, Tide, Monzo Business) | Free |
| Website | This site, hosted free on Netlify or GitHub Pages | £0 (+ about £10–£15/yr for a .co.uk domain) |
| Email and phone | Gmail, and a second SIM or WhatsApp Business | £0–£10/month |

### 6.2 Quality

- Every property gets a **property notes sheet** (key safe code, Wi-Fi, where supplies are kept, quirks) and the **reference photos** from your first visit.
- Every clean follows the **changeover checklist** (`templates/turnover-checklist.md`) and needs the required photos.
- **Spot checks:** visit or video-review about 1 in 10 cleans in the first few months.
- **Guarantee:** if a host reports a problem within 24 hours, a cleaner goes back and fixes it for free.
- **Targets:** 100% of cleans done before check-in · fewer than 2% complaints.

### 6.3 Covering every clean (the most important rule)

Fridays, Saturdays and Sundays are when most guests check out, so that's when you're busiest. A clean must **never** be missed.

- Have **at least two cleaners** who know each property, and give each property a main cleaner and a backup.
- Confirm the next day's schedule with cleaners every evening.
- You are the last-resort backup. Keep a basic cleaning kit ready.
- Don't take on more properties than your cleaners can cover on your busiest Saturday.
- **Bath-specific:** allow for city-centre parking and traffic. Know where cleaners can park (or load and unload) for each property, and include parking costs in your price where needed.

### 6.4 Supplies

- Cleaners use their own equipment (vacuum, mop, cloths).
- FreshTurn pays for consumables used at properties: cleaning products, bin bags, and restock items, which are recharged to the host. Budget about **£3 per clean**.
- Keep a small stock at home, or a lockable box at each property.

## 7. Working With Your Cleaners

### 7.1 The arrangement

Your cleaners stay **self-employed**: they work for you per job, just as they already do for their other clients. You **don't** employ them at the start.

To keep them genuinely self-employed:

- They can accept or decline jobs.
- They use their own equipment and can work for other people too.
- You pay per job against their invoice, not an hourly wage.
- They're registered as self-employed with HMRC and pay their own tax and National Insurance through Self Assessment.
- You tell them **what** a finished clean looks like (the checklist), not how to run their day.

> **When to get advice:** if a cleaner starts working mainly for you, on a schedule you set, can't send someone else in their place, or you supply all equipment and uniforms, HMRC may treat them as an employee. Then you'd need PAYE, holiday pay, a workplace pension and employer's liability insurance. Use HMRC's free "Check employment status for tax" tool, and talk to an accountant once you have more than 2–3 regular cleaners.

### 7.2 The written agreement

Use `templates/cleaner-agreement.md`. It covers:
- pay per job, and when you pay (weekly)
- the checklist and photo requirements
- confidentiality (key safe codes, client addresses)
- **non-solicitation:** they won't take FreshTurn clients directly for 12 months
- that they're self-employed and handle their own tax and insurance

### 7.3 Keeping them happy

- Pay on time every week, without fail.
- Give the same cleaners the same properties so they get faster and better.
- Give a £5 bonus for every clean that a guest or host mentions positively.
- Ask them what's annoying about each property and fix it with the host.

## 10. Legal, Tax & Insurance (UK)

- **Register as a sole trader** with HMRC for Self Assessment. You need to once you earn more than £1,000 a year from the business. It's free and done online. Becoming a **limited company** can make sense later, when profits grow; ask an accountant.
- **Business bank account.** Keep business money separate from personal money (Starling, Tide or Monzo Business are free or cheap).
- **Public liability insurance.** Covers you if a cleaner damages a client's property or someone is injured. It's essential and usually inexpensive for a small cleaning business. Also ask about **key cover** (lost keys and lock changes), which matters when you hold keys and key safe codes.
- **Employer's liability insurance** is a legal requirement only once you employ people, not while cleaners are self-employed.
- **Data protection (UK GDPR).** You'll hold names, addresses and key safe codes. Keep them secure, share them only with the cleaner who needs them, and check on the ICO website whether you need to pay the small annual data protection fee.
- **Client agreement.** Use `templates/client-agreement.md` for pricing, payment terms, cancellation, access and damage responsibility.
- **Cleaner agreement.** Use `templates/cleaner-agreement.md`.
- **Keys and codes.** Prefer key safes or smart locks with a code per cleaner. Never label keys with addresses.
- **Tax.** Set aside about 20–30% of your profit for Income Tax and National Insurance, and keep receipts for everything (supplies, mileage, phone).
- **VAT.** You don't need to register until your turnover passes the VAT threshold (£90,000 in 2025/26), likely sometime in year 2. Plan ahead, because VAT would apply on top of your prices.
- **Short-term let rules.** England has been bringing in new rules for short-term lets, including a planned national registration scheme and possible planning controls from Bath & North East Somerset Council. Tighter rules could mean fewer listings, so keep an eye on them.
- **Age.** If you're under 18, a parent or guardian will need to help register the business, sign contracts and open accounts.

> The templates are starting points, not legal advice. Have a solicitor review them before you rely on them.

## 11. Risks

| Risk | What to do about it |
|---|---|
| A cleaner doesn't show up | Two cleaners per property, evening confirmations, you as backup |
| A cleaner takes your clients | Non-solicitation clause, pay them well, and you own the relationship with the host |
| Something gets damaged or goes missing | Photo reports before and after, insurance, trusted cleaners only |
| Quiet winter (Bath is busiest spring–autumn, plus the Christmas Market) | Sell deep cleans in January–February; target lets that book all year |
| Weekend overload | Don't sign up more properties than you can cover on a Saturday |
| Parking and traffic in central Bath | Plan routes, know parking for each property, charge for parking where needed |
| Hosts pay late | Card on file, invoices due within 7 days |
| One big client leaves | Don't let one client be more than about 25% of your income |
| New short-term let rules reduce listings | Also offer end-of-tenancy and serviced-apartment cleans |
