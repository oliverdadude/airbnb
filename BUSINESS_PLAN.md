## 1. Executive Summary

**The business.** FreshTurn cleans Airbnb and short-term rental (STR) properties between guests. We do **not** manage listings, set prices or talk to guests. We handle the physical work: cleaning, beds and towels, restocking, and a photo report after every clean.

**The model.** The founder runs the business: winning clients, scheduling, quality control and billing. The cleaning is done by **independent cleaners the founder already knows and trusts**, paid a flat fee per job. That means:

- almost no startup cost (cleaners bring their own equipment)
- no payroll, so costs only exist when there's paid work
- the founder's time goes into growing the business, not scrubbing bathrooms

**The problem we solve.** Hosts who manage their own listings still have to deal with checkout day. Regular house cleaners often don't work weekends or same-day windows, don't restock or report damage, and nobody backs them up when they're sick. One bad clean can cost a host a 5-star review.

**The solution.** A reliable, calendar-synced turnover service with a checklist, a photo report after every clean, and a backup cleaner always available. It's priced per clean so hosts can cover it with the cleaning fee their guests already pay.

**Financial snapshot (base case):**

|---|---|---|

| Cleans completed | 672 | 2,250 |
| Revenue | $90,720 | $326,250 |

**Startup cost:** about **$2,700**.
**Break-even:** about **1 property**, because there are no fixed wages.

## 2. How the Business Works

```
 Host books a guest on Airbnb
          │
          ▼
 Calendar syncs to Turno ──► Clean job created automatically for checkout day
          │
          ▼
 Cleaner gets the job on their phone and accepts it
          │
          ▼
 Cleaner cleans between checkout (≈11am) and check-in (≈4pm)
 following the checklist, then uploads photos
          │
          ▼
 Host receives the photo report automatically
          │
          ▼
 End of month: FreshTurn invoices host ──► FreshTurn pays cleaners weekly
```

### Who does what

| Founder (you) | Cleaners |
|---|---|
| Find and sign up hosts | Do the cleaning to the checklist |
| Visit each property first and write its notes | Take the required photos |
| Run the schedule and make sure every clean is covered | Report damage, missing items and low supplies |
| Spot-check cleans; deal with any complaints | Cover for each other when possible |
| Bill hosts, pay cleaners, buy supplies | Invoice FreshTurn (or confirm completed jobs) weekly |
| Step in as backup cleaner in an emergency | |

## 3. Services & Pricing

### 3.1 What hosts get

| Service | Description |
|---|---|
| **Turnover clean** | Full clean after every checkout: kitchen, bathrooms, floors, beds made hotel-style, trash out, everything put back like the host's listing photos. |
| **Laundry** | *Phase 1:* cleaners wash the host's own linens and towels on site (most STRs have a washer). *Phase 2:* FreshTurn supplies its own linen sets for an extra fee (see 3.2). |
| **Restocking** | Toilet paper, soap, coffee and similar items topped up, billed at cost + 10%. |
| **Photo report** | 15–30 photos after every clean, plus any damage or missing items. That's the evidence hosts need for Airbnb damage claims. |
| **Deep clean** | Ovens, fridges, baseboards, windows. Offered seasonally or before a new listing goes live. |

### 3.2 Prices (what the host pays)

| Size | Turnover clean | + FreshTurn linens (Phase 2) | Deep clean |
|---|---|---|---|
| Studio | $85 | +$20 | $220 |
| 1 bed | $105 | +$25 | $260 |
| 2 bed | $135 | +$40 | $320 |
| 3 bed | $175 | +$55 | $400 |
| 4+ bed | from $220 | +$70 | from $500 |

**Extras:** extra bathroom +$20 · hot tub check +$25 · same-day rush (less than 4 hours' notice) +25% · restocking at cost + 10%.

### 3.3 What cleaners are paid (flat per job)

| Size | Cleaner pay | FreshTurn keeps (before supplies) |
|---|---|---|
| Studio | $45 | $40 |
| 1 bed | $55 | $50 |
| 2 bed | $70 | $65 |
| 3 bed | $90 | $85 |
| 4+ bed | $110+ | $110+ |
| Deep clean | 55% of price | 45% |
| Extra bathroom / rush | Cleaner gets 50% of the extra | 50% |

**Why these rates work:** a 2-bed clean takes one cleaner about 2.5–3 hours, so $70 is about $23–28 an hour. That's usually as good as or better than what they earn from house cleaning, and the jobs come to them without any effort. Check against what your cleaners charge now; they need to feel this is a good deal.

**Selling point to hosts:** most Airbnb hosts already charge guests a cleaning fee of $100–$200. *"Our clean costs about the same as your cleaning fee, so your guests effectively pay for it."*

## 5. Getting Clients (Marketing & Sales)

### 5.1 Channels, in order of how well they'll work at the start

1. **People you already know.** Friends, family and neighbours who rent out places, or who know someone who does.
2. **Local Airbnb host Facebook groups.** Search "[your city] Airbnb hosts". Answer questions, be helpful, and post your offer occasionally (see `templates/outreach-scripts.md`).
3. **Your existing cleaners' clients.** Some of the people they already clean for may own rentals.
4. **Google Business Profile.** Free. It makes you show up for "Airbnb cleaning near me". Ask every happy client for a review.
5. **The website.** Gives you credibility when hosts look you up, and lets them get a price and contact you.
6. **Turno marketplace.** List yourselves to get first clients and reviews.
7. **Partners (later).** Realtors who sell rental properties, co-hosts and handymen. Offer $50–$100 for every client they send.

### 5.2 Sales process

1. Reply to every enquiry the same day.
2. Visit the property for 20–30 minutes (free) and take reference photos of how it should look.
3. Send a quote within 24 hours.
4. **First clean at 50% off** so trying you is risk-free.
5. Connect their calendar, then send a photo report after every clean.
6. After 30 days, ask for a Google review and a referral.

## 6. Operations

### 6.1 Tools

| Need | Tool | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Scheduling, calendar sync, checklists, photos | **Turno** (lets you add your own cleaners) | about $10–$50/month |
| Invoicing and payments | Wave (free), QuickBooks or Square | Free – $35/month + card fees |
| Paying cleaners | Bank transfer, Venmo/Zelle (business account) or Gusto contractor payments | Free – $35/month |
| Website | This site, hosted free on GitHub Pages or Netlify | $0 (+ ~$15/yr for a domain) |
| Email and phone | Gmail / Google Workspace, Google Voice or a second SIM | $0–$10/month |

### 6.2 Quality

- Every property gets a **property notes sheet** (door code, Wi-Fi, where supplies are kept, quirks) and the **reference photos** from your first visit.
- Every clean follows the **turnover checklist** (`templates/turnover-checklist.md`) and needs the required photos.
- **Spot checks:** visit or video-review about 1 in 10 cleans in the first few months.
- **Guarantee:** if a host reports a problem within 24 hours, a cleaner goes back and fixes it for free. Whoever caused it isn't paid for the re-clean.
- **Targets:** 100% of cleans done before check-in · fewer than 2% complaints.

### 6.3 Covering every clean (the most important rule)

Saturdays and Sundays are when most guests check out, so that's when you're busiest. A clean must **never** be missed.

- Have **at least two cleaners** who know each property, and give each property a main cleaner and a backup.
- Confirm the next day's schedule with cleaners every evening.
- You are the last-resort backup. Keep a basic cleaning kit in your car.
- Don't take on more properties than your cleaners can cover on your busiest Saturday.

### 6.4 Supplies

- Cleaners use their own equipment (vacuum, mop, cloths).
- FreshTurn pays for consumables used at properties: cleaning products, bin bags, and restock items, which are recharged to the host. Budget about **$6 per clean**.
- Keep a small stock at home, or a lockable box at each property.

## 7. Working With Your Cleaners

### 7.1 The arrangement

Your cleaners stay **independent contractors**: self-employed people who work for you per job, just as they already do for their house-cleaning clients. You **don't** hire them as employees at the start.

To keep it a genuine contractor relationship:

- They can accept or decline jobs.
- They use their own equipment and can work for other people too.
- You pay per job, not per hour, against an invoice or a weekly job list.
- They handle their own taxes. In the US you'll send a 1099-NEC to anyone you pay $600+ in a year.
- You tell them **what** a finished clean looks like (the checklist), not how to run their day.

> **When to get advice:** if a cleaner starts working mainly for you, on a schedule you set, or you start supplying equipment and uniforms, the law may treat them as an employee. Talk to an accountant once you have more than 2–3 regular cleaners or someone works close to full-time for you. Rules vary by state and country.

### 7.2 The written agreement

Use `templates/cleaner-agreement.md`. It covers:
- pay per job, and when you pay (weekly)
- the checklist and photo requirements
- confidentiality (door codes, client addresses)
- **non-solicitation:** they won't take FreshTurn clients directly for 12 months
- that they're independent and handle their own taxes and insurance

### 7.3 Keeping them happy

- Pay on time every week, without fail.
- Give the same cleaners the same properties so they get faster and better.
- Give a $5 bonus for every clean that a guest or host mentions positively.
- Ask them what's annoying about each property and fix it with the host.

## 10. Legal & Insurance

- **Register the business.** An LLC (or your country's equivalent) separates your personal money from the business. Open a separate business bank account.
- **Insurance.** General liability at minimum, so you're covered if a cleaner breaks something in a client's home. Ask about a **janitorial bond**, which covers theft accusations. Ask cleaners whether they have their own insurance.
- **Client agreement.** Use `templates/client-agreement.md` for pricing, payment terms, cancellation, access and damage responsibility.
- **Cleaner agreement.** Use `templates/cleaner-agreement.md`.
- **Keys and codes.** Prefer smart locks with a separate code per cleaner. Never label keys with addresses.
- **Taxes.** Set aside about 25–30% of your profit for income tax, and keep receipts for everything.
- **Age.** If you're under 18, a parent or guardian will need to sign contracts, register the business and open accounts with you.

> The templates are starting points, not legal advice. Have a local lawyer review them before you rely on them.

## 11. Risks

| Risk | What to do about it |
|---|---|
| A cleaner doesn't show up | Two cleaners per property, evening confirmations, you as backup |
| A cleaner takes your clients | Non-solicitation clause, pay them well, and you own the relationship with the host |
| Something gets damaged or goes missing | Photo reports before and after, insurance, trusted cleaners only |
| Slow winter season | Sell deep cleans in quiet months; target homes that rent all year |
| Weekend overload | Don't sign up more properties than you can cover on a Saturday |
| Hosts pay late | Card on file, invoices due within 7 days |
| One big client leaves | Don't let one client be more than about 25% of your income |

## 12. Launch Plan

### Week 1–2: Check it'll work
- [ ] Sit down with your cleaners: explain the idea and the pay per job. Do they want weekend Airbnb work?
- [ ] Count the Airbnb listings near you (browse the Airbnb map, or check AirDNA / Inside Airbnb)
- [ ] Look at 20–30 local listings: what cleaning fee do they charge guests?
- [ ] Ask 3–5 local cleaners or companies what they'd charge for a 2-bed Airbnb clean
- [ ] Talk to 5 hosts: who cleans for them now, and what's annoying about it?
- [ ] Adjust prices if needed (website + section 3)

### Week 3–4: Set up
- [ ] Choose the name; check that the domain and Instagram handle are free
- [ ] Register the business, open a business bank account, get insurance
- [ ] Sign the cleaner agreement with each cleaner
- [ ] Put your details on the website and put it live (see README)
- [ ] Set up a Google Business Profile
- [ ] Set up a Turno account and add your cleaners

### Week 5–8: First clients
- [ ] Message everyone you know (`templates/outreach-scripts.md`)
- [ ] Join and post in local host Facebook groups
- [ ] Offer the first clean at 50% off
- [ ] **Goal: 2–3 properties**

### Week 9–12: Get good
- [ ] Time every clean; check your pricing still works
- [ ] Ask every client for a Google review
- [ ] **Goal: 5 properties**

### Market Research Checklist

- [ ] Number of Airbnb listings within 30 minutes of you
- [ ] Typical cleaning fee on local listings (your price should fit under it)

- [ ] What your cleaners currently earn per hour
